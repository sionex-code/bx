Chart 1 — Long-Term Cup / Rounding Bottom DASH/USD · multi-year

The dominant pattern on this chart is a large rounding bottom (often called a saucer or, with the recent price action, the start of a cup-and-handle). It formed over an extended bottoming period before the recent aggressive green candle pierced the rim of the pattern — and then printed a long upper wick rejecting off the neckline.

Recreated illustration of the chart structure. Yellow line = neckline resistance at ~$77.08. Red outline = the rounded "cup" base. Vertical dashed line = the textbook measured-move target from a confirmed breakout.

What the pattern actually says

A rounding bottom (or "saucer") is one of the longer-duration reversal patterns in classical TA. It's read as a slow transfer of supply → demand: sellers exhausted, buyers quietly accumulate, the curve flattens, then the trend changes. When the price escapes the rim with conviction (close above the neckline on strong volume), the canonical target equals the depth of the cup added to the breakout level.

What just happened on the chart

  1. Aggressive green breakout candle. It blew through the rim of the cup and printed a multi-month high.
  2. Immediate rejection. The next candle has a very long upper wick that probed above the rim and got sold back down to ~$64. That's textbook "first test back of broken resistance."
  3. Currently resting just below the neckline after the rejection. Needs either (a) another push that closes a full candle above $77 with volume, or (b) a multi-week consolidation forming the "handle" of a cup-and-handle.

Why the rejection candle matters more than the breakout candle
A breakout that fails on the very first retest is one of the most common sources of bull-trap losses in classical TA. The setup is not "confirmed" until the neckline is retested as support and holds on a closing basis. Treating the breakout as live before that confirmation is the single biggest avoidable mistake here.

Market-structure read

  1. Prior uptrend broke. The yellow line marks an area that was previously support during an earlier rally. Price rallied into it, failed, and decisively lost the green-dotted base.
  2. Breakout → retest → failed retest. The yellow level re-tested from below acted as resistance — classic "polarity flip."
  3. Breakdown of the green base. Once the green-dotted support gave way, the lower timeframe shifted to a clear downtrend (lower highs, lower lows).
  4. Cascading lower to a new swing low.
  5. Bounce attempt visible in the right-most green cluster — currently a single small candle, not yet a trend change.

How the two charts relate

Reading them together

The two charts are the same asset at different zoom levels. Chart 1 shows the long-term story (multi-year accumulation → breakout attempt). Chart 2 is the granular tape showing the chop and the recent breakdown. Together they frame a single narrative:

  • Long-term: a slow accumulation cup with a recent breakout attempt that got rejected at the rim.
  • Short-term: a market still digesting that breakout, currently mid-range between broken support and overhead supply.
The big question is whether the short-term bounce resolves into a successful retest of $77 (cup-and-handle continuation) or rolls over and re-tests the $40s.

Trade Rules, Invalidations & Risk Management

If you're trading the breakout

  • Trigger: weekly candle close > $77 with above-average volume.
  • Invalidation: weekly close < $41.
  • Risk per unit: entry – stop = $77 – $41 ≈ $36.
  • Reward per unit (measured target): $135 – $77 ≈ $58.
  • Risk/reward ratio: ≈ 1.61 R — workable but not exceptional; size accordingly.

If you're trading the bounce from the recent low

  • Trigger: reclaim of the green-dotted ~$65 area, ideally with a 4H close back above and rising volume.
  • Invalidation: a closing break of the recent low cluster.
  • Stop: just below the lower low of the new swing.
  • Targets: first — the green-dotted support (now resistance) at ~$65; second — the yellow supply at ~$85.

Universal rules of engagement

Risk-management essentials (these matter more than the pattern)
  • Size each position to a pre-defined risk % of total capital (most professionals risk 0.5–1.5% per trade).
  • Never use leverage that turns a normal stop into a liquidation.
  • Move stops to breakeven only after price proves structure, not on first green candle.
  • Treat the breakout as unconfirmed until the close, the volume, and a retest all agree.
  • If the catalyst (DASHCON, narrative rotation, ETF rejection) fades, the pattern argument weakens; size down.

What could break the bullish read

  • Sustained weekly close < $41.
  • Loss of the multi-year higher-low structure.
  • Privacy-coin regulatory shock (EU AML rules expanding, more exchange delistings).
  • Macro risk-off move led by BTC that drags DASH down regardless of its own structure.

What could break the bearish read

  • Successful reclaim of the green-dotted ~$65 with conviction.
  • Then a retest and hold of the yellow ~$85 supply.
  • Privacy-coin narrative rotation continuing (Oct 2025 onward) on macro liquidity tailwinds.
